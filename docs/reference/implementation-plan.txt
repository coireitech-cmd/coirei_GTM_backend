6. CORE TRUTHFULNESS REQUIREMENT
============================================================

THIS IS NON-NEGOTIABLE.

The system must NEVER manufacture factual information.

The engineering target is:

ZERO UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS.

A crawler cannot guarantee that the information published on the
Internet is itself truthful.

Therefore the system's responsibility is:

DO NOT STATE A FACT THAT THE SYSTEM CANNOT SUPPORT WITH EVIDENCE.

If sufficient evidence cannot be found:

RETURN:

UNKNOWN

or:

NOT_PUBLICLY_VERIFIED

Do not guess.

Do not estimate.

Do not extrapolate.

Do not fabricate.

Do not invent.

Do not convert vague statements into precise numbers.

Do not use generic industry assumptions to fill missing company data.

Examples:

If ARR cannot be verified:

ARR = UNKNOWN

If valuation cannot be verified:

VALUATION = UNKNOWN

If employee count cannot be verified:

EMPLOYEE_COUNT = UNKNOWN

If annual sales for a product cannot be verified:

ANNUAL_PRODUCT_SALES = UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN is always preferable to an unsupported answer.

============================================================
7. FACT VS INTERPRETATION
============================================================

The system must maintain a strict distinction between:

WHAT A SOURCE STATES

and

WHAT THE SYSTEM DERIVES.

Example:

Source:

"We serve more than 500 customers."

The system may store:

customer_claim = "more than 500 customers"

It must NOT silently transform that into:

customer_count = 500

unless the evidence actually supports that representation.

Example:

Source:

"We raised $20M."

This means:

funding_raised = $20M

It does NOT automatically mean:

valuation = $20M

Example:

Source:

"We serve enterprises."

This does NOT mean:

customer_count = UNKNOWN

unless the field is simply unavailable.

Do not make unsupported logical jumps.

11. CONFIDENCE SYSTEM
============================================================

Every extracted factual observation should receive a confidence score.

Use a consistent numerical scale.

Recommended initial scale:

0.00 → 1.00

The final methodology must be proposed and approved.

Confidence may consider:

- Source quality
- First-party vs third-party
- Direct statement vs derived information
- Number of corroborating sources
- Source freshness
- Specificity
- Extraction certainty
- Agreement between sources
- Historical relevance

Example:

0.95+
    Strongly verified

0.80–0.94
    High confidence

0.60–0.79
    Medium confidence

Below threshold
    Unverified / insufficient evidence

These thresholds are examples.

Do not finalize them without reviewing the methodology.

IMPORTANT:

Confidence must NEVER be used to convert unsupported information into
a fact.
